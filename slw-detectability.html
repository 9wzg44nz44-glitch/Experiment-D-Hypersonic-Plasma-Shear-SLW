Hypersonic plasma sheath · Hively EED · TinySA Ultra · first honest numbers

Could a Dan-class receiver hear an SLW / SW from a hypersonic sheath?

A first quantitative estimate. Sheath parameters come from published reentry data and textbook plasma physics (FACT). The SLW/SW launch and range law are Hively's EED, used exactly as printed (HYP). Every coupling nobody has measured is a slider swept over decades (SWEEP), never a single value. Modelling choices made here are marked ASSUMPTION.

Bottom line (baseline, 100 km, TinySA Ultra with no LNA, 30 kHz RBW). On Dan's actual bands (433.92 / 1296 MHz Hively-style antennas) the HYP SLW is not detectable for any η ≤ 1 under the Ohm's-law (v×B) sheath-current estimate, even at ne = 1020 m⁻³ with full current closure (ηth ≥ 17). Only a large resonant low-frequency SLW antenna (≈1 MHz, λ²/4π aperture) gets ηth ≈ 0.025 at ne = 1018. That is detectable only if η > 0.025, and the FACT-side inputs alone make that threshold uncertain by roughly ±2–3 decades. η is the fraction of the in-band sheath current that is SLW-active. η = 1 means the sheath is as SLW-efficient as Hively's own balun antenna. The classical passive signatures (the TEM from the same currents, and sheath thermal emission) sit at least ≈110 dB below the floor at 100 km for the v×B estimate (≥ 70 dB even for the extreme diffusion bound).

Assumptions (explicit)

  1. ne is an independent input (10¹⁴–10²⁰ m⁻³). RAM-C II anchors only the ≈7.6 km/s case: electrostatic probes at 1–7 cm aft read 10¹⁴–10¹⁸ m⁻³ from 85 to 53 km (Jones & Cross, TN D-6617), reflectometers covered 10¹⁶–10¹⁹ m⁻³ (Grantham, TN D-6062), and CFD gives a 61 km stagnation peak of ≈1.9×10²⁰ m⁻³. Lower Mach generally means far lower ne, which is not modelled.
  2. ν scales with USSA-1976 neutral density from Grantham's 2.51×10⁹ s⁻¹ at 45.72 km, with a ×0.1…×10 slider.
  3. The shear-layer current is Ohm's law with the geomagnetic motional field. Full closure is the upper bound. The diffusion option is an extreme bound (ambipolar ⇒ net ≈ 0).
  4. Turbulent cells have correlation length ℓc = δ and random phases. δn/n = δJ/J. The spectrum is flat to S/2π, then f−5/3, with no dissipation cutoff (optimistic at 433/1296 MHz).
  5. Equivalent-sinusoid Ipk = √2 Irms, fed into Hively's Eq. B5, which is printed without a ½ time-average factor (±3 dB ambiguity).
  6. Far field (kr ≫ 1). No path loss between the sheath and the receiver (neutral air σ is negligible; ionospheric paths are not modelled). Line of sight is assumed. A ground receiver needs r ≥ altitude.
  7. The SLW receive aperture is either the hub SleeveBalunSNR convention (π(5 cm)²·0.5) or λ²/4π (reciprocal isotropic, needs a resonant antenna ≈ λ/4 long, e.g. ≈75 m at 1 MHz).
  8. The Hively RX rejects TEM (ambient noise and classical signals) by SE dB (default 55 dB, the hub's "sealed" tent value). The SLW is assumed to pass the Faraday isolation unattenuated (HYP).
  9. Classical TEM: a Hertzian-dipole sum inside the plasma, then slab escape (normal incidence). Thermal: an optically thick slab at Te.
  10. The SW proxy uses the same η as the SLW (the same C source), with an energy flux of c·C²/2μ₀ (C obeys □C = source ⇒ speed c).

Detectability verdict (with uncertainty)

  • 433.92 / 1296 MHz Hively-style receivers + TinySA (no LNA): not detectable at ≥10 km for any η ≤ 1 under the v×B estimate (ηth ≥ 1.7 at 10 km and ≥ 17 at 100 km, even with ne = 10²⁰ and full closure). The only exception is the unphysical unbalanced-diffusion bound at ne = 10²⁰, where ηth ≈ 0.1–1 at 100 km.
  • 30 MHz, hub aperture: ηth ≈ 570 (ne 10¹⁸) and ≈ 5.7 (10²⁰) at 100 km. Not detectable.
  • ≈1 MHz, large resonant SLW antenna (λ²/4π): detectable only if η > 0.025 (ne 10¹⁸) or η > 2.5×10⁻⁴ (10²⁰) at 100 km. The FACT-side uncertainty in that threshold is roughly ±2–3 decades: closure 3 decades, shear 2.5, ne ±2, δ −1…+1.9, δn/n −0.7…+1, ν ±1 (Fig. 6).
  • SW proxy: needs η·√κC ≥ √2·ηth. No SW receiver exists, so κC is unconstrained.
  • Classical: the TEM from the same currents and the sheath's thermal emission are at least ≈110 dB below the floor at 100 km for the v×B estimate (≥ 70 dB for the diffusion bound; up to hundreds of dB under full blackout) (FACT). The sheath is "seen" classically only by radar, optical/IR, and blackout of its own telemetry.
  • Higher shear helps only by pushing fc up. J depends on Δu = S·δ, which is capped at the flight speed, so a thinner layer at the same Δu lowers the current. "Shear as amplifier" holds only at fixed δ.
